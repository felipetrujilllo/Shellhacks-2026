# Relay

Relay reads two utilities' public transmission plans, finds the cross-border projects that
sit close together, and ranks them as coordination opportunities on an interactive map.

Live: **https://relaygrid.us** (also at `https://gridwatch-b3trj.ondigitalocean.app`, the
DigitalOcean default host).

[VIDEO: under-3-minute demo: map loads, click the rank 1 pair, detail panel with distance,
time gap and savings, zoom out to the savings total]

## Inspiration

FERC Order No. 1920 (2024) exists because neighboring utilities have historically planned
transmission in isolation. Dominion Energy South Carolina (DESC) and Georgia Power share the
Savannah River border and both publish their plans, but nobody can compare them side by side:
one is a 44-page PDF of project sheets, the other a 668-page IRP volume. We built Relay to
answer one question: which of these projects should the two utilities be talking about?

## What it does

- Maps every located DESC and Georgia Power project, one color per utility, each drawn as a
  line between its two end substations. Low-confidence locations get their own style.
- Flags every cross-utility pair whose project centers are less than 25 miles apart
  (haversine) and records the gap between their in-service dates in days.
- Ranks the flagged pairs as top coordination opportunities, re-sortable by score, distance,
  time gap or estimated savings. Clicking a pair zooms the map to it and opens a detail
  panel with both projects, the numbers, and a savings estimate that explains itself.
- Takes your own projects as a CSV upload and ranks them with the published pairs. The
  server stores nothing; Relay keeps your uploads in your browser.

The live site flags **39 pairs** among **115 located projects**. The top pair is DESC's
Jasper – Okatie 230 kV #2 line and Georgia Power's McIntosh – Purrysburg 230 kV reactors:
**5.66 mi** apart, **152 days** between in-service dates, score 83%, estimated savings
**$709,579**. Estimates across all 39 pairs total **$4,242,766**. (Source: live
`/api/overlaps` and `/api/projects`, 26 Sep 2026, 22:42 EDT.)

## How we built it

1. **Parse.** `pdfplumber` reads DESC's one-page project sheets and Georgia Power's Table 2,
   joined to its per-project sheets on the TEAMS number: name, in-service or need date, and
   cost where published.
2. **Split.** A rule pipeline turns "Okatie-Bluffton 115kV: Rebuild" into two substation
   names, with a short override list for irregular names.
3. **Locate.** We cached named `power=substation` features from Overpass (by operator tag,
   plus untagged South Carolina substations) and committed them, so the demo never depends
   on Overpass. Exact name matches win over a conservative fuzzy match, and each project is
   marked `confirmed` or `low`.
4. **Center.** Midpoint of the two located endpoints, or the one endpoint if only one is
   found, as the sponsor specifies.
5. **Overlap engine.** A deterministic Python batch job compares every DESC center with every
   Georgia Power center using haversine (R = 3958.8 mi) and flags pairs under 25 mi. Score =
   0.6 × closeness + 0.4 × time proximity, with the time gap capped at five years. No LLM.
6. **Store.** The loader writes projects (PostGIS point and line geometries) and flagged
   pairs to Tiger Data (Postgres + PostGIS) in one transaction.
7. **Serve.** FastAPI serves `/health`, `/projects`, `/overlaps`, `/overlaps/{id}` and
   `POST /workspace`, with a 503 when the database is unreachable. React, Vite, TypeScript,
   Tailwind and MapLibre GL draw the UI.
8. **Deploy.** One DigitalOcean App Platform app: frontend at `/`, API at `/api`. GitHub
   Actions redeploys only after CI passes on `main`, then smoke-tests the live API.

**Savings model.** Estimated savings = 5% of the known project cost × distance factor × time
factor, computed by the API for each pair.

- **5%** is an assumption, not a sourced figure: the mobilization and crew overhead two
  coordinated jobs could share (rules of thumb put mobilization at 3–10%).
- The distance factor falls linearly from 1.0 at 0 mi to 0.1 at 25 mi. The time factor
  halves for every 365 days between in-service dates.
- DESC publishes costs and Georgia Power redacts them, so every published pair is estimated
  from the DESC cost. If both costs are known, the smaller one is used. If neither is, the
  estimate is null with the reason ("cost redacted in Georgia Power IRP").

**Golden test:** the sponsor's 10-project starter table reproduces exactly its 6 overlaps at
the same distances, and the smoke test checks that all 6 sponsor pairs are served live.

## Data & limitations

From `docs/coverage.md`, generated by the same build that produces the dataset:

| Utility | Parsed | Located | Confirmed | Low | Excluded |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DESC | 44 | 28 | 4 | 24 | 16 |
| Georgia Power | 208 | 87 | 35 | 52 | 121 |
| **Total** | **252** | **115** | **39** | **76** | **137** |

- **137 of 252 projects are not on the map.** 129 have no endpoint that matches an OSM
  substation, 6 match only a substation well inside the other utility's state, and 2 match
  several far-apart substations. We list them rather than guess.
- **`low` is common.** It means at least one endpoint is uncertain: no operator tag, a
  hand-entered coordinate, or a missing endpoint. We audited the top 10 pairs against the
  PDFs, and all 11 projects in them held up (`docs/data_audit.md`).
- **Georgia Power costs are redacted**, so savings rest on the DESC side only.
- **Centers are approximate.** One audited DESC Okatie substation project is centered on the
  line it folds in, not on the substation. That is an open team decision.

## Challenges

- **PDFs, not tables.** Georgia Power's Table 2 wraps names and breaks them at hyphens, so we
  took names from the per-project sheets.
- **Names are not locations.** "GOSHEN" matches two Georgia Power substations more than 80
  miles apart; we pick the one nearest the line's other end. "BUZZARD ROOST" matched a dam
  substation in South Carolina and paired four Atlanta projects with a DESC line, so matches
  deep in the other utility's state are now rejected.
- **Thin OSM tagging for DESC.** Most of its substations have no operator tag, and Okatie has
  no named substation in OSM, so it gets a manual override with a cited source.
- **Four people, one codebase, 36 hours.** One test gate (backend, frontend, smoke) kept the
  demo branch deployable.

## Accomplishments

- The sponsor's reference table is reproduced exactly, and all 6 sponsor pairs appear in the
  full live dataset.
- Every located project comes from the utilities' own filings. Nothing is synthetic.
- Every savings figure shows its calculation, and pairs with no known cost show no number
  rather than an invented one.

## What's next

- **Plain-English savings notes with Snowflake Cortex.** We built and tested a module that
  has Cortex rewrite each pair's numbers as one or two sentences, checks every dollar figure
  against the deterministic estimate, and falls back to the estimate's own explanation. It is
  not wired into the API or the UI yet.
- Locate more of the 137 excluded projects with more OSM sources and cited overrides.
- Add a third utility, such as Santee Cooper or another SERTP member.
- Ingest new filings as utilities publish them.
- Replace the assumed 5% with real right-of-way and crew data.
